previous record's hash, and appends it to the one authoritative store; every owned tool call routes
through it, and the chain verifies after a restart.

JC-5. A single helper is the DRY requirement; a persisted chain head is what makes verification survive
a restart (M-6).

</objective>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| owned tool -> audit store | Every tool call crosses here; the record must be complete and chained. |
| audit store -> verifier | The chain is only tamper-evident if the head is persisted and verification recomputes hashes. |
| untrusted complaint -> audit record | Untrusted text is serialised through one typed helper, never concatenated. |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-03-11 | Tampering | audit chain | high | mitigate | Each record hashes its canonical fields plus `prev_hash`; `verify()` recomputes the whole chain and reports the first break (AUDIT-01, D-08). |
| T-03-12 | Repudiation | approval record | high | mitigate | Approval records capture the named approver and role from the validated token, so a queue write is attributable (AUDIT-02). |
| T-03-13 | Tampering | chain head on restart | high | mitigate | The chain head is persisted in the authoritative store, so a restart or backfill cannot silently invalidate the chain (M-6). |
| T-03-14 | Injection | audit serialisation | medium | mitigate | Records are serialised through `canonical_json`, never string-concatenated, so untrusted complaint text cannot break log parsing (M-13). |
| T-03-15 | Tampering | tool-call coverage | high | mitigate | ONE uniform interceptor wraps every owned tool, so no tool call escapes the audit (AUDIT-01, AUDIT-03). |
